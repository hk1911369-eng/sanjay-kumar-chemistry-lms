# JMT Academy — HSC 2027 Chemistry Course Blueprint

**Status:** Draft for academic review. Not a published course, official syllabus document, or student-facing teaching resource.  
**Target cohort:** HSC 2027 (Bangladesh), Science group  
**Subject codes:** Chemistry 1st Paper 176; Chemistry 2nd Paper 177  
**Language plan:** Bengali explanations with standard English scientific terminology, symbols and equations; optional English-medium notes after teacher review.

## 2. Course outcomes

By the end of the course, a learner should be able to:

1. Apply laboratory safety, hazard recognition, measurement and responsible waste-handling practices.
2. Interpret qualitative chemical observations and use evidence to identify common ions or substances within the prescribed syllabus.
3. Explain periodic trends, electronic structure and chemical bonding using models and evidence.
4. Apply chemical equilibrium, energetics, reaction rates and related quantitative reasoning to chemical changes.
5. Connect chemistry with practical, industrial, environmental and economic contexts.
6. Explain atmospheric, water and environmental chemistry using balanced equations and data.
7. Recognise organic functional groups, represent structures and reason about reaction families.
8. Solve mole, concentration, stoichiometry and titration problems with correct units and significant figures.
9. Explain electrochemical cells, electrode processes and redox relationships.
10. Interpret selected chemical-industry processes, raw materials, products, safety and environmental impacts.

## 3. Proposed chapter-to-module map

The lesson names below are a teaching sequence proposal. They are not claimed to be a verbatim NCTB subchapter list. Teachers must adjust scope and terminology to the prescribed textbook.

### Chemistry 1st Paper

#### Chapter 1 — Safe Use of Laboratory
- 1.1 Laboratory orientation, conduct and risk awareness
- 1.2 Hazard labels, chemical storage and personal protective equipment
- 1.3 Safe handling of glassware, heating and common apparatus
- 1.4 Measurement, observations, records and laboratory notebook
- 1.5 Waste segregation, spill response principles and emergency procedures
- 1.6 Practical orientation and safety competency check

**Practice:** apparatus identification, hazard-symbol matching, risk-assessment scenarios, notebook-format exercise. No unsupervised experiments or dangerous chemical procedures.

#### Chapter 2 — Qualitative Chemistry
- 2.1 Qualitative versus quantitative analysis; evidence and inference
- 2.2 Sample preparation, solubility and systematic observation
- 2.3 Tests for syllabus-relevant anions
- 2.4 Tests for syllabus-relevant cations
- 2.5 Flame tests and characteristic observations, where prescribed
- 2.6 Confirmatory tests, interference and limitations
- 2.7 Unknown-sample reasoning using observation–inference tables

**Practice:** teacher-reviewed observation tables, simulated unknown-sample cases and practical viva. Wet-lab work must be supervised and restricted to approved school-laboratory procedures.

#### Chapter 3 — Periodic Properties of Elements and Chemical Bonds
- 3.1 Atomic structure, electronic configuration and periodic-table organisation
- 3.2 Periodic trends and their explanations
- 3.3 Ionisation energy, electron affinity and electronegativity
- 3.4 Ionic and covalent bonding; Lewis representations
- 3.5 Molecular shape, polarity and intermolecular interactions
- 3.6 Bond properties and structure–property reasoning
- 3.7 Mixed conceptual and numerical problem-solving

#### Chapter 4 — Chemical Changes
- 4.1 Chemical change, thermochemical language and energy profiles
- 4.2 Reaction rate and factors affecting rate
- 4.3 Reversible reactions and dynamic equilibrium
- 4.4 Equilibrium shifts and Le Chatelier's principle
- 4.5 Acid–base concepts, pH and indicators, where prescribed
- 4.6 Oxidation–reduction and balancing redox equations
- 4.7 Integrated numerical and creative-question practice

#### Chapter 5 — Vocational Chemistry
- 5.1 Chemistry in daily life and local industries
- 5.2 Materials, products and process selection
- 5.3 Industrial quality control and measurement
- 5.4 Safe production, handling and storage
- 5.5 Pollution prevention, waste minimisation and sustainability
- 5.6 Case study: a Bangladesh-relevant chemical or materials process

The academic lead should select case studies that match the textbook and can be supported by reliable, citable sources. Do not imply endorsement of a company or product.

### Chemistry 2nd Paper

#### Chapter 1 — Environmental Chemistry
- 1.1 Composition and chemistry of the atmosphere
- 1.2 Air pollutants, sources, reactions and effects
- 1.3 Water quality, dissolved substances and contamination
- 1.4 Greenhouse effect, climate-related chemistry and ozone chemistry
- 1.5 Gas laws and their chemical applications, where prescribed
- 1.6 Pollution monitoring, prevention and environmental problem-solving

#### Chapter 2 — Organic Chemistry
- 2.1 Carbon bonding, structural representation and classification
- 2.2 Homologous series, nomenclature and isomerism
- 2.3 Hydrocarbons and their characteristic reactions
- 2.4 Functional groups and important organic families
- 2.5 Reaction mechanisms and orientation concepts only to the depth prescribed
- 2.6 Organic compounds in daily life, materials and industry
- 2.7 Reaction maps, conversions and synthesis reasoning

#### Chapter 3 — Quantitative Chemistry
- 3.1 Relative atomic/molecular mass and the mole concept
- 3.2 Chemical formulae, equations and stoichiometric relationships
- 3.3 Limiting reagent, yield and purity calculations
- 3.4 Solutions and concentration units
- 3.5 Dilution and solution preparation calculations
- 3.6 Volumetric analysis and titration calculations
- 3.7 Mixed numerical sets with units, significant figures and error checks

#### Chapter 4 — Electrochemistry
- 4.1 Oxidation, reduction and electron transfer
- 4.2 Electrochemical cells and cell notation
- 4.3 Electrode potentials and cell potential
- 4.4 Electrolysis and Faraday-related calculations, where prescribed
- 4.5 Batteries, corrosion and prevention
- 4.6 Applications and interpretation of electrochemical data

#### Chapter 5 — Economic Chemistry
- 5.1 Chemical industries, raw materials and value chains
- 5.2 Selected industrial chemical processes in the prescribed text
- 5.3 Fertiliser, glass, cement, paper or other textbook-relevant industries
- 5.4 Product quality, process efficiency and cost factors
- 5.5 Occupational safety and environmental impacts
- 5.6 Bangladesh-focused industrial case study and data interpretation

## 4. Teaching pattern for every chapter

Each chapter should follow a consistent LMS structure:

1. **Chapter orientation:** prerequisites, learning outcomes, estimated study time and diagnostic quiz.
2. **Concept lessons:** short teacher-led video or live class, original notes, diagrams drawn by JMT and worked examples.
3. **Guided practice:** scaffolded MCQs, short-answer checks and stepwise numerical problems.
4. **Creative-question workshop:** stimulus interpretation and structured answer planning; model answers must be original and teacher-reviewed.
5. **Practical or simulation:** only where relevant; clearly distinguish a virtual demonstration from a real supervised laboratory activity.
6. **Chapter assessment:** mixed MCQ, numerical and creative-question practice with explanations.
7. **Revision:** summary sheet, formula/reaction map, spaced-retrieval quiz and remediation for common misconceptions.

Suggested lesson duration: 8–15 minutes for a focused concept video, with longer live problem-solving sessions as required. Duration is a design suggestion, not an official requirement.

## 5. Assessment blueprint

Use the assessment pattern specified by the latest official board notice for the student's examination year. Until verified, keep the following as configurable LMS assessment types rather than hard-coded mark allocations:

- Diagnostic quiz (low stakes)
- Topic MCQ practice with explanations
- Numerical problem set with method marks
- Creative-question practice (stimulus, knowledge, understanding, application and higher-order reasoning)
- Practical observation record and viva
- Timed paper-level mock exam
- Cumulative spaced-revision test

Assessment quality controls:
- Every question has a chapter/topic tag, difficulty label, answer key, explanation and source/author record.
- Numerical answers are independently recalculated and unit-checked.
- MCQs have one defensible best answer and plausible, misconception-based distractors.
- Creative questions use original stimuli and a teacher-approved marking rubric.
- Practical questions distinguish expected observations from actual learner observations.
- No question is labelled “board question” unless the exact past-paper source and year have been verified.

## 6. Practical and virtual-lab plan

Create practical activities only after the lab teacher confirms local apparatus, reagents, supervision and risk controls. Start with:
- Laboratory apparatus and safety identification
- Observation/inference table interpretation
- Qualitative-analysis decision-tree simulation
- Solution concentration and dilution calculations
- Titration data analysis using synthetic datasets
- Electrochemical-cell diagram and data interpretation

A virtual activity must be labelled **simulation** and must not be represented as a substitute for required hands-on practical assessment.
